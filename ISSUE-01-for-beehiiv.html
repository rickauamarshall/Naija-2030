Super Eagles Tracker — Issue №1

The Origin Issue

Why This Exists

Nigeria has missed the last two World Cups. Not for lack of talent — for lack of anyone publicly keeping score of who's actually available, who's close to being lost to another federation, and who the NFF should already be calling. That's the gap this project fills: a public, weekly-updated depth chart of the best available Super Eagles pool, including the uncapped and dual-national players most fans have never heard of. Independent. Not affiliated with the NFF, CAF, or FIFA. Built to pressure the federation with receipts, not vibes.

The NFF Story So Far

Late August: NFF president Ibrahim Musa Gusau and the entire Executive Committee resigned. President Tinubu ordered the National Sports Commission to run a full reform — governance, electoral structure, development pathways.

Since then: FIFA and CAF sent a joint fact-finding mission to Abuja, consulting NFF stakeholder groups (Players' Union, coaches' association, league bodies, referees' association). Four of the five groups consulted reportedly preferred a Normalisation Committee over a Caretaker Committee, and stakeholders have reportedly agreed in principle to a normalisation committee running the federation for up to a year, built from an eight-member structure the National Sports Commission recommended to FIFA. Committee members would be barred from contesting the eventual election themselves.

The final call sits with FIFA's Council now, based on the mission's report. As of this writing, no public decision has landed — still "expected in the coming days," which is what everyone was saying a week ago too. This is moving slower than the news cycle wants it to.

The name to watch: Seyi Olofinjana — Chelsea's Head of Scouting for Africa, ex-Super Eagle, already in our own staff directory — is being reported as the firm favorite to take the NFF presidency. If that holds, it's a genuinely rare combination: a real domestic playing career plus a real, current European executive job, not a purely political pick.

We'll have a clean update the moment FIFA actually rules.

The Dual-National Battle — Where Things Actually Stand

We're not going to pretend every one of these is a clean win-in-progress. Some genuinely are. Some just got worse.

  • 🟢 David Ozoh (Derby County, 21, born Valencia) — agreed to switch to

Nigeria after meeting Chelle toward the end of last season. This isn't breaking news, despite how it first got reported to us — he doesn't need FIFA clearance, just a letter of release from the English FA (he's uncapped by England at every level). Chelle's hoping he's cleared before the November window, initially at U23 level for the AFCON U23 qualifiers.

  • 🔴 Lesley Ugochukwu (Galatasaray, on loan from Burnley) — a real

setback here: France's U21 side named him in their squad for 2027 Euro U21 qualifying on Sept 18, despite July reports he'd started pursuing a Nigerian passport. No official change-of-association step has actually happened. His Nigeria tie is through his uncle, former Super Eagle Onyekachi Apam — real, but currently losing, not winning.

  • 🟢 Arthur Okonkwo (Wrexham GK, born Camden) — FIFA approved his

switch back in April 2026 (paperwork delays cost him the 2025 AFCON), and he's a regular call-up now. Older news than it might sound, but still one of our clearest wins.

  • 🔴 Honest Ahanor (Crystal Palace, on loan from Chelsea) — two senior

friendly caps for Italy already (vs. Luxembourg and Greece), but at 18 with only 2 caps he remains eligible for FIFA's one-time-switch rule. Real, formal mechanism — not just hope.

  • 🔴 Jeff Ekhator (Juventus) — made his senior Italy debut vs. Greece

on June 7, 2026 (a friendly, with an assist), so he's not uncapped. But with only 1 cap and well under 21, he's still eligible for the same one-time-switch mechanism as Ahanor.

  • 🔴 Luca Koleosho, Michael Kayode — Italy is reportedly moving to lock

in both as they rebuild.

  • 🔴 Noah Atubolu (Freiburg GK) — omitted from Germany's actual World

Cup squad, and still hasn't played a competitive senior match for Germany, so his path to Nigeria technically remains open. The door isn't closed, but it isn't being pushed on either.

  • 🟡 Samuel Aghehowa (Porto) — one competitive appearance for Spain,

but FIFA's one-time-switch rule (under-21, ≤3 caps) keeps him eligible for Nigeria. Eguavoen reportedly has family ties on his mother's side.
