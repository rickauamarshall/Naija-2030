The Origin Issue

Nigeria has missed the last two World Cups. Not for lack of talent — for lack of anyone publicly keeping score. That's what this is: a weekly, receipts-first look at the players Nigeria could call on and the federation trying to reform itself around them. Independent. Not affiliated with the NFF, CAF, or FIFA. Let's get into it.

Headlines

  • The NFF's entire Executive Committee resigned in late August. FIFA and CAF sent a joint fact-finding mission to Abuja; four of five stakeholder groups consulted preferred a Normalisation Committee over a Caretaker Committee.
  • Stakeholders have reportedly agreed in principle to an eight-member Normalisation Committee running things for up to a year. FIFA's Council still hasn't ruled — full story below.
  • The name to watch: Seyi Olofinjana, Chelsea's Head of Scouting for Africa and already in our staff tracker, is being reported as the firm favorite for the NFF presidency.
  • Housekeeping, said plainly: we pulled 17 entries off the Big Board this week. They turned out to be fabricated or badly misattributed — placeholder names from early prototyping that never got a real research pass. Board's smaller now (12 entries) and every name on it is independently verified or clearly marked TBD. Smaller and honest beats bigger and fake.
  • Next up: Madagascar (Fri, Sept 25, Uyo) and Guinea-Bissau (Tue, Sept 29) — AFCON 2027 qualifiers — then a Russia friendly Oct 6. Full squad breakdown next issue.

Feature of the Week: Everyone Agreed. Nobody's In Charge.

Here's the strange part of the NFF story that's easy to miss under the headlines: there is, as far as anyone can tell, no actual disagreement left. The board resigned. The National Sports Commission wants a Normalisation Committee. Four of the five NFF stakeholder groups FIFA consulted — Players' Union, coaches, league bodies, referees — want the same thing. FIFA and CAF sent a joint mission, spent four days in Abuja, and left with what sounds like consensus in hand. And three-plus weeks later, nobody has actually been put in charge of anything.

I don't think this is a story about conflict. It's a story about a gap between agreement and action, and that gap is where I'd be watching if I were Nigerian football right now.

Worth grounding this in what actually happens once a normalisation committee gets installed, rather than guessing. Kenya's football federation has been through this twice — a 2004 FIFA suspension resolved in about two months once a committee (chaired by Kipchoge Keino) was actually seated, and a longer 2022 suspension that took roughly nine months. Two very different timelines, but both moved once the committee existed. The lesson isn't "normalisation is fast" or "normalisation is slow" — it's that the clock barely starts until someone's actually appointed. Nigeria isn't suspended the way Kenya was either time; this is a voluntary reform process, not a ban, which arguably removes the external pressure that got Kenya's committees seated as fast as they were.

So the honest read is: the slow part isn't the reform, it's the approval. FIFA's Council has the mission's report. Nobody's said no. Nobody's said yes either. We'll have a clean update the moment that changes — that's really the whole point of tracking this weekly instead of writing one big piece and moving on.

Eligibility Watchlist

Where the dual-national cases actually stand, not where the hope says they stand:

  • 🔴 Rio Ngumoha (Liverpool, 17) — the most urgent case in the pool right now, not a hypothetical one. Already made his senior England debut (a friendly, didn't cap-tie him), but Tuchel has since called him into England's competitive Nations League squad. Three appearances there and he's gone for good.
  • 🟢 David Ozoh (Derby County, 21) — agreed to switch to Nigeria last season, not "this week" like it first got reported to us. Needs an FA release letter, not FIFA clearance. Chelle's hoping he's cleared before November, initially at U23 level.
  • 🔴 Lesley Ugochukwu (Galatasaray, loan from Burnley) — real setback: named in France's U21 squad Sept 18, despite July reports he'd started pursuing a Nigerian passport. No official change-of-association step has happened.
  • 🟢 Arthur Okonkwo (Wrexham GK) — FIFA approved his switch back in April. Older news than it sounds, but still one of the clean wins.
  • 🔴 Honest Ahanor (Crystal Palace, loan from Chelsea) — two senior friendly caps for Italy, but at 18 with only 2 caps he's still eligible for FIFA's one-time-switch rule.
  • 🔴 Jeff Ekhator (Juventus) — already made his senior Italy debut (June 7, friendly). Not uncapped anymore, but the same one-time-switch mechanism as Ahanor still applies.
  • 🔴 Luca Koleosho, Michael Kayode — Italy reportedly moving to lock in both as they rebuild.
  • 🔴 Noah Atubolu (Freiburg GK) — omitted from Germany's actual World Cup squad, still hasn't played a competitive senior match for them. Door's open, not being pushed on.
  • 🟡 Samuel Aghehowa (Porto) — flagging this one as genuinely unresolved: different sources gave us 1, 2, and 4 caps for Spain, and if his senior debut really was a competitive Nations League match in Nov 2024, the age math on FIFA's one-time-switch window gets tight fast. Needs a real verification pass before we say anything more definite. Not adding to the board until that happens.
