# NAIJA 2030 — Issue №2
**September 22, 2026**

## Squad Week: Chelle Names Two Very Different Groups

Three matches in twelve days. Two AFCON 2027 qualifiers that actually matter, and one friendly that's really an audition.

**Fri, Sept 25 — Nigeria vs. Madagascar** (Godswill Akpabio Stadium, Uyo, 4:00 PM WAT)
**Tue, Sept 29 — Guinea-Bissau vs. Nigeria** (away, Bissau)
**Tue, Oct 6 — Nigeria vs. Russia** (friendly, Nizhny Novgorod, 6:00 PM Nigerian time)

Both AFCON qualifiers are Group L, Matchday 1 and 2 — a group that includes co-host Tanzania, where only the best non-host finisher joins Tanzania in qualifying. Start well or the group gets very unforgiving, very fast.

## The AFCON Squad — Chelle Plays It Straight

For the games that count, Chelle went with his strongest available group: Osimhen, Lookman, Iwobi, Chukwueze and Nwabali headline it, with Ndidi as captain. Recalls for Maduka Okoye, Calvin Bassey, Bright Osayi-Samuel, Frank Onyeka, Akor Adams, Tolu Arokodare and Semi Ajayi. Ola Aina is back after missing recent camps; Benjamin Fredricks returns from injury.

Osimhen's inclusion is the one to watch — he's carrying a groin/adductor issue that was expected to cost him 2–3 weeks. Chelle called him up anyway, "closely monitoring" his condition. If he's not right, this is a very different squad by kickoff.

Three uncapped debutants made the cut: **Isaac James** (Portugal-based defender), **George Ilenikhena**, and **Moses Usor** — both forwards. None of the three were on our radar before this week, which is worth admitting plainly rather than pretending our board is more complete than it is. We're adding all three to the tracker this issue.

## The Russia Squad — This Is the Audition

For the Oct 6 friendly, Chelle named a **deliberately different group.** Osimhen, Lookman, Iwobi and Chukwueze are all rested. In their place: Kelechi Iheanacho gets a recall, and three more uncapped names get a first look — **Victory Akpe** (Switzerland-based defender), **Samuel James** (Finland-based goalkeeper), and **Abdullahi Bewene** (Czechia-based fullback). Ndidi captains again; Moses Simon and Nwabali are the senior heads in a young room.

This is exactly the "Big Board" thesis in miniature: a low-stakes friendly used to stress-test the tier below the headline names. We're watching all three debutants.

## Where Our Board Has Gaps — Said Plainly

Cross-checking this week's call-ups against our own pool: **none** of the six brand-new names above (James, Ilenikhena, Usor, Akpe, James again, Bewene) were already tracked here. That's a real gap, not a footnote — a site built to say "we know this pool better than the NFF does" has to own it when the NFF's own coach finds players first. We're treating this as a standing to-do, not a one-time fix.

The players who *were* already on our board and got minutes this window: Kelechi Iheanacho (Lookman's listed depth), Frank Onyeka (Ndidi's), and Zaidu Sanusi (a starting DEF). Small confirmation the board isn't just guessing.

## How Wide Is Everyone Else's Net?

We talk about Nigeria's pool as deep and under-scouted. Worth asking: how does that compare to teams that actually made the last World Cup?

**USA — the long funnel.** Mauricio Pochettino evaluated more than 60 candidates across 19 months, 8 training camps, and 24 matches before naming his final 26. That's not a squad announcement, that's a multi-year audition process with a paper trail. Nigeria's entire "wider pool" apparatus, by comparison, is a WhatsApp-speed news cycle and this newsletter.

**Italy — the active poacher (and the cautionary tale).** Roberto Mancini built a deliberate pipeline of *oriundi* — dual nationals, mostly Argentine and Brazilian-Italian — explicitly to get to them before other federations did. Mateo Retegui is the headline case: Argentina-developed, Italian citizenship through a grandmother, now a senior Azzurri forward. That's the exact playbook Italy is currently running *against* Nigeria — Honest Ahanor, Jeff Ekhator, Luca Koleosho and Michael Kayode are all being chased by the same federation that invented the modern version of this move. And here's the twist worth sitting with: despite all that talent-pipeline discipline, Italy is not automatically at the World Cup — they're in the playoffs, same as ever. Pool depth is not destiny. Administration and moments still matter more.

**Germany — losing on both ends.** Post-Euro 2024 retirements (Kroos, Gündogan) left Nagelsmann thin on caps even before Neuer un-retired to patch the gap. Germany's dual-national scouting is real too — Hawaii-born, German-heritage defender Noahkai Banks reportedly turned down a USMNT call-up for a Germany pathway this year. Nigeria isn't just competing with Italy for players like Ahanor; it's one federation in a genuinely global scramble.

The honest takeaway: **Nigeria's talent pool is not the problem.** The gap is process — Pochettino's 19-month paper trail versus our own admitted blind spots on six names this week alone.

## The NFF Story — Where the Reform Actually Stands

Quick, sourced update since Issue №1:

- FIFA and CAF sent a joint fact-finding mission to Abuja and completed consultations with NFF stakeholder groups (Players' Union, coaches' association, league bodies, referees' association). Four of the five groups consulted reportedly preferred a **Normalisation Committee** over a Caretaker Committee.
- Stakeholders have reportedly **agreed in principle** to a normalisation committee running the federation for up to one year, with an eight-member structure recommended by the National Sports Commission to FIFA. Committee members would be barred from contesting the eventual election themselves.
- The final call now sits with FIFA's Council, based on the mission's report. As of this writing, no public decision has landed yet — expected "in the coming days," which is what we said in Issue №1 too. This is moving slower than the news cycle wants it to.
- **The name to watch:** Seyi Olofinjana — Chelsea's Head of Scouting for Africa, ex-Super Eagle, and already featured in this site's staff directory — is being reported as the **firm favorite** to take the NFF presidency. If that holds, it's a genuinely interesting outcome: someone with both a real playing career and a real, current European executive job, rather than a purely political appointment.
- One loose thread: there are conflicting reports about whether a September 27 elective congress goes forward under the *old* structure, or whether it's superseded entirely by the normalisation process. We're not resolving that here — flagging it as unresolved rather than picking a version that sounds cleaner than the reporting actually is.

We'll have a clean update the moment FIFA rules either way.
