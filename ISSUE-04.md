# Super Eagles Tracker - Issue No. 4

## Russia, Release Letters, and the Experimental Squad Nigeria Almost Had

*Russia friendly preview - draft for publication today, Oct. 5, 2026*

Nigeria wanted an experimental friendly. Nigeria got one, but not in the clean way an ambitious federation should want.

The useful version of Tuesday's Russia match was obvious: take a low-stakes window, test the edges of the pool, give Eric Chelle live evidence on players outside the automatic XI, and leave with a clearer depth chart. Instead, the official story going into Nizhny Novgorod is already about absences. The NFF announced a 24-man squad on Sept. 17. By Oct. 4, it was explaining why club restrictions, injuries, and one permit issue had reduced the group to 18. [NFF original squad](https://thenff.com/international-friendly-ndidi-simon-nwabali-lead-super-eagles-to-nizhny-novgorod-for-russia/) [NFF reduced squad](https://thenff.com/international-friendly-club-restrictions-injuries-weaken-nigeria-ahead-of-russia-clash/)

That is still a preview. It is just also a process story.

## What Changed

The original Russia list had a clear idea behind it. It mixed senior leaders like Stanley Nwabali, Wilfred Ndidi, Moses Simon, Kelechi Iheanacho, Raphael Onyedika, Bright Osayi-Samuel and Akor Adams with first-time or fringe names: Samuel James, Victory Akpe, Chibuzo Nwoko/Nwoke, Stephen Michael, Igor Wilfred, Ridwan Abdulrasaq, Suleiman Mubarak, Peter Edokpolor, Abubakar Umar and Abdullahi Umar. [NFF original squad](https://thenff.com/international-friendly-ndidi-simon-nwabali-lead-super-eagles-to-nizhny-novgorod-for-russia/)

Then the squad bent out of shape.

NFF says Ndidi was knocked out by a knock from the Guinea-Bissau qualifier. Akpe is injured. Onyedika, Osayi-Samuel, Ridwan Abdulrasaq and Usor were stopped by their clubs. Mubarak could not travel because his resident permit was not secured. Ibraheem Ajasa of FC Rostov was expected as a late invitee. [NFF reduced squad](https://thenff.com/international-friendly-club-restrictions-injuries-weaken-nigeria-ahead-of-russia-clash/)

That leaves a different kind of experimental squad: not the 24-man test Chelle selected, but the 18-man group that survived the administrative reality of the trip.

## Is This Normal?

Some of it, yes.

International football always has friction with clubs. Players carry knocks. Clubs worry about minutes, travel, return schedules, insurance and medical risk. A friendly against Russia, at the end of a long September-October window, is exactly the kind of fixture where club pressure can show up.

But FIFA's rules also make the broad principle clear: clubs are obliged to release eligible players when called by their association for international windows listed in the calendar, and the September-October window from 2026 is a 16-day window that can include up to four matches. The same annexe says associations must notify players and clubs in writing at least 15 days before the first day of the window, clubs must confirm release within six days, and requests for FIFA help must arrive at least five days before the match. [FIFA RSTP, Annexe 1](https://cdn.fifa.sportsagentinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/25163043/FIFA_Status-and-Transfer-of-Players_July_2025_EN.pdf)

That is the uncomfortable part. If the first Russia squad was announced Sept. 17 and the match is Oct. 6, the calendar did not sneak up on anyone. Either some clubs resisted despite the window, or some call-up, travel, documentation or escalation steps were not fully nailed down early enough. From the outside, we cannot prove which piece failed for each player. We can say the outcome does not look like a high-functioning release operation.

The fairest line is this: one or two late club fights are normal. Losing enough players that the federation has to publish a "club restrictions, injuries weaken Nigeria" explainer two days before kickoff is not something a serious evaluation program should treat as routine.

## What This Friendly Could Have Been

There is a useful comparison happening across the Atlantic. The Athletic framed Mauricio Pochettino's recent USMNT friendlies around a simple idea: he went young, he went fresh, and the young players gave him a real selection problem. Cavan Sullivan's goalscoring record, Mathis Albert breaking an earlier Christian Pulisic mark, and Julian Hall scoring in each of his first three senior appearances changed the conversation around the American pool almost immediately.

That is what a low-stakes friendly can do when the right players are actually in the building. It can make the next roster harder to pick in a good way.

That is the missed opportunity risk for Nigeria. A Russia friendly did not need to be glamorous to be useful. It could have given Chelle a clean look at fringe defenders, dual-national midfield cases, and next-cycle attackers before another urgent window. Instead, the thinness of the traveling squad means the match may tell us as much about who was available as who is ready.

## Four Players We Would Have Liked To See

Pending club clearance, fitness and player willingness, here is one name per position band who would have made this friendly more useful.

### Goalkeeper: Maduka Okoye

If Russia was going to be part evaluation, part reset, Okoye would have been the cleanest goalkeeper test. Nwabali is already known. Samuel James is the long-view prospect. Okoye is the senior-pool question that still needs current evidence: where he sits in the hierarchy when Nigeria is not simply defaulting to the AFCON memory.

### Defender: Chibuike Nwaiwu

Nwaiwu is a better fit for this kind of friendly than a lot of emergency defensive depth. He is 22, playing senior football at Trabzonspor, already had a provisional Super Eagles connection, and still has no senior debut. If Nigeria wants a 2030-cycle center-back ladder, this is the sort of name that should get a controlled look before the stakes rise.

### Midfielder: David Ozoh

Ozoh is the exact profile that should turn a friendly into a recruitment mechanism: young, Nigeria-eligible, born in Spain, raised in England's system, and not yet tied at senior level. Even if the paperwork or release letter is not finished, he is the kind of case where the NFF should be able to show progress, not just interest.

### Forward: Zadok Yohanna

This is the fun one. Yohanna gives the issue a future-facing edge without pretending he is ready-made for a senior tournament tomorrow. Brighton signed him after an AIK breakout; he is young, direct and exactly the type of wide forward whose first senior camp could build attachment before another federation or club pathway shapes the story for him.

## What To Watch Tuesday

Start with the new names, but do not overreact to one friendly. Samuel James, Benjamin Fredrick, Abdullahi Bewene, Chibuzo Nwoke, Stephen Michael, Igor Wilfred, Peter Edokpolor, Abubakar Umar and Abdullahi Umar are not all the same kind of prospect. Some may be genuine discoveries. Some may simply be available bodies in a disrupted camp.

That distinction matters. An experimental squad is only useful if the federation learns something specific from it.

For Chelle, the questions are simple:

- Can any fringe defender handle senior international tempo?
- Does the midfield have anyone who can progress play, or is the experiment just more running?
- Can Akor Adams, Kelechi Iheanacho or one of the new forwards make the striker picture less dependent on Osimhen's availability?
- Does Samuel James look like a real senior goalkeeper prospect or simply a depth invite?

For the NFF, the question is sharper:

If this friendly was supposed to be planned, why did so much of the plan fail so late?

Rise, Eagles, Rise. One Pool. FWC30 Qualification at All Costs.
