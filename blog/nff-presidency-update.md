# The NFF Presidency Update: Still Stuck, But Not Static

*Super Eagles Tracker — independent, fan-built. Not affiliated with the NFF, CAF, or FIFA. Written September 23, 2026.*

---

We promised this one in Issue №2's Instagram caption, so let's not bury the answer: FIFA's Council has **still not ruled** on a Normalisation Committee for the NFF. If you came here hoping for a name, a start date, or a signed order, we don't have one, and neither does anyone else reporting on this as of today. That's the honest headline, and it's worth sitting with for a second before we get into what *has* moved, because "nothing decided yet" and "nothing happening" turn out to be two different things.

## What's actually confirmed

The skeleton of the story hasn't changed since Issue №1, and it's worth restating plainly because it's the part we're most confident in:

- The NFF's Executive Committee, led by then-president Ibrahim Musa Gusau, resigned on **August 27, 2026**.
- FIFA formally accepted those resignations around **September 1**, and said it was backing a broader reform process for Nigerian football.
- FIFA and CAF sent officials to Abuja for consultations with stakeholder groups, in more than one round — reporting puts the most recent phase of that mission wrapping up around **September 15**.
- The NFF's elective congress, originally set for **September 27 in Lafia**, was suspended pending the outcome of this process. (Small, slightly grim detail: that's the same weekend as the Madagascar qualifier — two Nigerian football stories that were never actually going to be separate.)
- As of today, no FIFA Council or Bureau ruling on a Normalisation Committee has been announced, and no committee members have been named.

None of that is new information. It's the same "expected in the coming days" holding pattern Issue №2 already flagged over a week ago, and it's still true today.

## What's new — and it complicates the "everyone agreed" framing

Issue №1 described this as a story with no real disagreement left: the board gone, four of five stakeholder groups consulted by FIFA preferring a Normalisation Committee over a caretaker arrangement, consensus in hand. Reporting from the mission's second phase (roughly September 14–16) sharpens that picture in a way we think is worth correcting toward, not softening.

The four groups backing normalisation — the Players' Union, coaches, referees, and (per more recent reporting) the Sports Writers Association of Nigeria, plus a bloc of former NFF presidents and general secretaries — are still aligned. **But the fifth group, the State Football Association chairmen, isn't just a mild holdout.** Multiple outlets report they've told FIFA and CAF directly that there is "no crisis" in Nigerian football, that the existing NFF Congress is capable of filling the leadership vacuum itself, and that an externally imposed Normalisation Committee isn't warranted.

Here's why that matters more than a simple 4-to-1 vote count suggests: the State FA chairmen aren't a fifth voice with a fifth of the say. Under the NFF's reformed electoral structure (adopted this past March), the 36 state associations plus the FCT reportedly still control around **68.5% of the total voting power** in the NFF Congress — down from roughly 84% under the pre-reform structure, but still comfortably a supermajority on their own. We're flagging that percentage as reported, not independently audited by us against NFF statute text — different outlets frame the before/after numbers slightly differently, and we'd rather say that plainly than present a clean number we haven't verified ourselves.

So the actual shape of the disagreement isn't "one outlier group being stubborn." It's the bloc with the most institutional votes pushing back against the bloc FIFA's own mission appears to be leaning toward siding with. That's a real standoff, not a formality waiting to be signed off. It's a more honest description of where this is stuck than "everyone agreed" — and we'd rather update our own framing in public than let an earlier issue's tidier version stand uncorrected.

## The presidency race: a name got firmer, not final

Seyi Olofinjana — Chelsea's Head of Scouting for Africa, already in our staff tracker — was reported as the front-runner back in Issue №1. The more recent reporting (mid-to-late September) upgrades that language to "firm favourite," and gives a reason why: outlets are reporting that the National Sports Commission's originally preferred candidate for the role reportedly **withdrew from consideration**, said to be over concerns it would conflict with his personal or business interests. We have not been able to independently confirm who that original candidate was — the reporting we found names Olofinjana as the beneficiary of the withdrawal without consistently naming who stepped back, and we're not going to guess at a name to fill that gap.

Worth being precise about what this is and isn't: Olofinjana has not been appointed to anything. There is no NFF presidential election scheduled — that process is on hold along with everything else pending the Normalisation Committee question, and reporting suggests FIFA has indicated Normalisation Committee members themselves would be barred from contesting a subsequent election, which is itself still a reported detail, not something we've seen in a FIFA document directly. "Firm favourite" is a real and repeated characterization across Nigerian sports press. It is not a result.

## What we're not going to pretend we know

A few things that would genuinely move this story, that haven't happened:

- **No specific date** has been reported for when FIFA's Council or its Bureau will actually rule. "In the coming days" has now been circulating for close to two weeks across multiple outlets, which should tell you what that phrase is worth.
- **No committee has been seated.** Reported plans describe an eight-member body, nominated by the NSC and vetted by FIFA, running for something between six months and a year — but that's a proposal under discussion, not a done deal, and we're treating every detail of it (size, duration, vetting process) as reported-not-confirmed until an actual FIFA statement names it.
- **No presidency process has formally opened.** Whatever timeline eventually applies to Olofinjana or any other name, it starts after the Normalisation Committee question resolves, not before.

## What we think this means

Our read from Issue №1 was that the gap between agreement and action was the real story, more than any actual conflict. Three weeks on, we think that needs a footnote: the "gap" wasn't just bureaucratic lag — it's a live disagreement between the stakeholder groups that want reform imposed and the bloc that holds most of the institutional vote and doesn't want it imposed on those terms. FIFA sitting on this isn't inertia. It's FIFA holding two incompatible asks from people who all technically count as "Nigerian football stakeholders," and deciding which one it's willing to override.

That's a harder problem than "everyone agrees, someone just needs to sign off," and it's also a more honest one. We'd rather tell you the process is contested and slow than tell you it's settled and just delayed.

We'll keep tracking this the same way we track eligibility cases and squad news: what's confirmed stays confirmed, what's reported stays labeled as reported, and the moment there's an actual FIFA ruling — a committee named, a date set, a president elected — that's the next update, not a paragraph buried in a squad-news issue.
