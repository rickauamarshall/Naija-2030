# Social editorial workflow

Super Eagles Tracker uses X and Instagram as reporting and distribution channels, not as a substitute for verification. The feeds are an editorial radar: they help us identify developments worth checking, then shape timely posts, site updates, and newsletter coverage.

## Source watch

Monitor, in priority order:

1. Official NFF, Super Eagles, CAF, FIFA, club, and federation accounts.
2. Player and coach accounts when they are the direct source of an announcement.
3. Reputable reporters and football outlets for developments that still need confirmation.
4. Community accounts for leads only, never as the sole source for a factual claim.

An official post can establish that an announcement was made. It does not, by itself, prove eligibility, injury severity, transfer status, or an editorial conclusion.

## Editorial intake labels

Each useful development should be assigned one label before it is reused:

- **Verified announcement:** directly supported by an official source or primary document.
- **Reported development:** published by a reputable secondary source and still attributable.
- **Needs confirmation:** a lead that should not become a factual assertion yet.
- **Editorial angle:** a defensible interpretation of verified information.
- **Potential newsletter item:** material that deserves fuller context than a social post allows.

Eligibility changes remain permanently human-gated. No account post, feed item, or automated classifier may move a player to OPEN, WATCH, or TIED without human review and a recorded source.

## Platform roles

### X

X is the rapid-response and conversation channel. Use it for official announcements, concise matchday observations, verified stats, short analysis, threads, and links to the site and newsletter. Keep posts readable, use American English, avoid unnecessary hashtags, and use no more than two hashtags on a standard post. Do not chase unrelated trends or repeat near-identical promotional posts.

### Instagram

Instagram is the visual and evergreen channel. Use it for squad and fixture graphics, carousel explainers, site and pitch-view screenshots, matchday recap cards, and short reels or Stories when there is useful visual material. Use the Beehiiv link in the profile and direct readers there from captions and Stories. Prefer the SET logo, original graphics, site captures, and properly attributed official material where permitted. Avoid third-party player or club imagery unless usage rights are clear.

## Repurposing loop

The newsletter is the weekly source of considered analysis:

1. Monitor official and reputable feeds.
2. Verify and label meaningful developments.
3. Publish timely, source-linked X commentary when appropriate.
4. Turn the strongest development into an Instagram visual or carousel.
5. Synthesize the week in Beehiiv with more context and caveats.
6. Update the site or pool only after the underlying data and editorial decision are reviewed.

The same fact can appear on all three channels, but the copy should be adapted to the platform. Do not copy a newsletter paragraph into a social caption or turn a social rumor into newsletter fact.

## Matchday workflow

Before kickoff, prepare a short watch list from official squad and fixture information. During and after the match, record only confirmed events and licensed-provider statistics. Afterward, publish a concise X update, use Instagram for a visual recap if the material warrants it, and reserve broader interpretation for the next newsletter.

## Approval boundaries

- No live X or Instagram post without project-owner approval for that post or planned batch.
- No newsletter send without project-owner approval.
- No automatic eligibility, nationality, or federation-status claims.
- No implied endorsement or official status from NFF, CAF, FIFA, a club, or a player.
- Every meaningful correction should be reflected in the relevant site, newsletter, and social draft before the next publication.
